# Scoring and Comparison

## 1. Purpose

The comparison system helps a player understand how two journeys differed. It does not rank real hospitals, prove causation, estimate real clinical effectiveness, or announce a universal winner.

AESIVIA should prefer a **journey evidence profile** over a single score.

## 2. Comparison eligibility

Two runs form a controlled comparison only when they share:

- PatientDefinition and initial patient state;
- DiseaseDefinition/version and initial hidden parameters;
- RunSeed and consequential random-stream rules;
- scenario and ruleset versions;
- exogenous event schedule at the declared equivalence level; and
- equivalent player decisions wherever both runs offer equivalent choices.

If player decisions, content versions, or scenario conditions differ, the pair is labeled **exploratory comparison** and the divergence is highlighted.

## 3. Metric families

### 3.1 Clinical outcome

- endpoint class: recovery trajectory, stabilized/ongoing care, deteriorated, or death;
- broad stability at endpoint;
- modeled injury/burden band;
- complication occurrence and timing;
- intervention response band; and
- residual-care intensity band.

These are fictional simulation outputs, not prognostic measures.

### 3.2 Timeliness

- symptom onset to help-seeking action;
- arrival to first clinical contact;
- first contact to diagnostic acquisition;
- acquisition to interpretation;
- interpretation to responsible-recipient acknowledgment;
- recognition to treatment decision;
- decision to treatment start; and
- total wait, movement, and active-care time.

Metrics should show distributions or timestamps, not pass/fail guideline colors until standards are clinically and locally validated.

### 3.3 Information flow

- number of patient facts requested repeatedly;
- number of material corrections by the patient;
- handoffs and information items transferred;
- critical result routing latency;
- receipt-to-acknowledgment latency;
- stale, missing, duplicated, or superseded information events;
- number of actors holding the current key finding at each stage; and
- unresolved information dependencies.

### 3.4 Safety and reliability

- safety-relevant exceptions;
- time to detect and time to recover;
- missed or late escalation opportunities within the simulation;
- identity discrepancies and resolution;
- resource conflicts;
- rework caused by incomplete or incorrect information; and
- deterioration events detected versus noticed late.

Avoid a binary “safe/unsafe hospital” label from one run.

### 3.5 Patient experience and effort

- patient-initiated requests for help;
- repeated history/information burden;
- number and duration of relocations;
- unexplained waits;
- understanding/trust state at key decisions;
- time separated from available support person, if modeled;
- accessibility/communication friction; and
- decisions made under impaired capacity or distress.

### 3.6 Staff work and coordination

- staff actions and active work minutes by capability;
- interruptions and task switches;
- handoff/communication actions;
- coordination and documentation actions;
- duplicated or abandoned work;
- queue pressure at key moments;
- after-hours/escalation workload if modeled;
- journey-coordination overhead; and
- reliance on informal workarounds.

### 3.7 Resource use

- diagnostic and treatment resource occupancy;
- transport movements;
- reserved-but-idle time;
- rework resource consumption;
- peak simultaneous demand; and
- capacity blocked by incomplete dependencies.

No financial cost metric belongs in v0.1 unless a credible cost model is separately designed and validated.

## 4. Presentation hierarchy

### Layer 1 — human summary

A short, neutral paragraph: outcome, major waits, significant system moments, and uncertainty.

Example style: “In this run, treatment began earlier after several dependencies were prepared in parallel. The patient still experienced a complication generated by the shared disease seed. This simulation cannot determine whether the timing difference caused the endpoint.”

### Layer 2 — aligned journey timeline

Two horizontal timelines aligned to onset and arrival, showing:

- patient location and felt state;
- active care versus waiting;
- findings and who knew them;
- handoffs;
- exceptions/recovery;
- decision points; and
- intervention and outcome.

### Layer 3 — evidence profile

Metric families shown as separate cards or small charts. Use raw measures and plain-language interpretation. No total score.

### Layer 4 — simulation audit

For advanced review: seed/version data, player-decision divergence, stochastic opportunities, modeled causal links, and limitations.

## 5. No-winner rule

The game does not calculate “Hospital A: 63, Hospital B: 88.” A composite score would hide trade-offs, exaggerate model validity, and encourage gaming.

The comparison may state bounded observations such as:

- shorter/longer in this run;
- fewer/more repeated requests;
- earlier/later acknowledgment;
- lower/higher staff-action count;
- exception recovered/not recovered; or
- same/different simulated endpoint.

It must add “in this run” and expose any decision or configuration divergence.

## 6. Counterfactual boundaries

Same-seed replay is a designed counterfactual experiment, not proof of what would happen to a real patient. Once hospital workflows diverge, the set and timing of opportunities can diverge too. Therefore:

- identical keyed opportunities may reuse identical draws;
- hospital-specific opportunities are explicitly marked;
- events absent from one run have no fabricated counterpart;
- the report avoids subtracting outcome probabilities as if they were observed evidence; and
- causal claims are limited to mechanisms represented by the simulation.

## 7. Patient decision differences

The comparison categorizes choices as:

- `Equivalent`: same meaningful choice in both runs;
- `Unavailable`: hospital state did not present an equivalent choice;
- `ChangedByPlayer`: the player chose differently;
- `ChangedByCapacity`: PatientState made the original choice unavailable; or
- `PresentationOnly`: wording differed but consequence class was the same.

Decision divergence does not invalidate the run, but may downgrade it from controlled to exploratory.

## 8. Fairness diagnostics

The internal review view should compare:

- starting workload/capability;
- staff budget and actions;
- clinical service availability;
- physical travel-time budget;
- disease and patient stream identity;
- exogenous operational event equivalence;
- treatment-effect model identity; and
- author-forced versus emergent exceptions.

Any unexplained asymmetry blocks claims about workflow differences.

## 9. Sensitive outcome presentation

After death or severe deterioration:

- pause before showing analysis;
- use non-celebratory visual/audio treatment;
- avoid points, rank, confetti, or “try again to win” language;
- provide an option to exit or view the debrief later;
- distinguish player actions from system and disease factors; and
- avoid blame.

## 10. v0.1 minimum comparison set

The first build needs only:

- endpoint class;
- seven key timestamps;
- total waiting, movement, and active-care time;
- handoff count;
- repeated-information count;
- critical finding route/acknowledgment time;
- safety-relevant exceptions and recovery time;
- patient help requests and unexplained waits;
- staff action/communication/coordination counts; and
- player-decision divergence.

Additional metrics should be deferred unless they answer a clear design question.

## 11. Validation questions

- Do players understand why the journeys differed without being told which hospital to prefer?
- Can reviewers reconstruct every displayed metric from the event log?
- Does the report remain meaningful when both clinical outcomes are identical?
- Does it remain respectful when the patient dies?
- Can Hospital A outperform B on some process metrics without appearing to break the premise?
- Are model limitations visible before users overinterpret the comparison?
